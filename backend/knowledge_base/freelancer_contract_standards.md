# Freelancer Contract Standards

## Purpose

Freelancer contracts establish the legal relationship between an independent contractor and a client. A well-drafted freelancer agreement reduces disputes, clarifies expectations, allocates risk, and ensures fair compensation.

This document describes generally accepted standards, best practices, and risk indicators commonly found in freelancer agreements.

---

# Freelancer Relationship

A freelancer is generally an independent contractor and not an employee.

Indicators of a freelance relationship include:

* Freedom to determine work methods.
* Ability to work for multiple clients.
* Payment based on deliverables or milestones.
* No employee benefits.
* No guaranteed ongoing work.
* Control over working hours.

Contracts that exert excessive control over a freelancer may increase the risk of worker misclassification.

---

# Scope of Work

The scope of work should clearly define:

* Services to be provided.
* Deliverables.
* Project objectives.
* Technical requirements.
* Milestones.
* Deadlines.

## Best Practice

The agreement should specifically identify deliverables rather than relying on broad descriptions.

Example:

Good:

"Design and deliver a responsive marketing website consisting of five pages."

Poor:

"Provide all services requested by the client."

## Risk Indicators

High Risk:

* Undefined scope.
* Open-ended obligations.
* Broad discretionary authority granted to the client.

Medium Risk:

* Vague descriptions.
* Missing specifications.

---

# Deliverables

Deliverables should be objectively measurable.

Examples include:

* Source code.
* Design files.
* Reports.
* Marketing assets.
* Written content.
* Research documents.

## Best Practice

Each deliverable should include:

* Description.
* Format.
* Delivery date.
* Acceptance criteria.

---

# Project Timeline

A contract should establish:

* Start date.
* Milestone dates.
* Final delivery date.

## Best Practice

Timelines should account for:

* Client feedback periods.
* Revision cycles.
* Delays caused by client inaction.

## Risk Indicators

High Risk:

* Freelancer responsible for delays caused by client.

Medium Risk:

* No timeline specified.

---

# Acceptance Criteria

Acceptance criteria define when work is considered completed.

## Best Practice

Contracts should include a review period.

Example:

"Client shall review delivered work within seven business days. Failure to provide written objections during this period shall constitute acceptance."

## Risk Indicators

High Risk:

* Payment dependent upon subjective client satisfaction.
* No acceptance process.

---

# Revision Policy

Contracts should define:

* Number of revisions.
* Revision scope.
* Additional revision fees.

## Best Practice

Specify a reasonable number of revisions.

Example:

"Two rounds of revisions are included."

## Risk Indicators

Critical Risk:

* Unlimited revisions.

High Risk:

* Revisions at client discretion without compensation.

---

# Payment Terms

Payment clauses are among the most important sections.

Contracts should specify:

* Total project fee.
* Milestone payments.
* Hourly rates.
* Retainers.
* Currency.
* Taxes.

## Best Practice

Use milestone-based payments.

Example:

* 50% advance.
* 25% upon milestone completion.
* 25% upon final delivery.

## Risk Indicators

Critical Risk:

* Payment entirely dependent on client discretion.

High Risk:

* No payment deadline.
* No invoice process.
* Payment after indefinite approval.

---

# Advance Payment

Advance payments reduce freelancer risk.

## Industry Standard

Common structures include:

* 30% advance.
* 50% advance.
* Full advance for small projects.

## Risk Indicators

Medium Risk:

* No advance payment.

High Risk:

* Significant upfront work required before payment.

---

# Late Payment Protection

Contracts should address:

* Payment deadlines.
* Interest on overdue payments.
* Collection costs.

## Best Practice

Invoices should be payable within:

* 7 days.
* 15 days.
* 30 days.

## Risk Indicators

High Risk:

* No late payment protection.

---

# Client Responsibilities

Clients should be responsible for:

* Providing information.
* Providing approvals.
* Providing access to systems.
* Timely communication.

## Best Practice

Client-caused delays should extend project deadlines.

## Risk Indicators

High Risk:

* Freelancer liable for delays caused by client.

---

# Intellectual Property

Intellectual property ownership should be explicitly defined.

## Common Approaches

### Assignment After Payment

Ownership transfers after full payment.

This is generally freelancer-friendly.

### Immediate Assignment

Ownership transfers immediately upon creation.

This is generally client-friendly.

### License Model

Freelancer retains ownership while granting usage rights.

---

# Portfolio Rights

Freelancers often wish to display completed work.

## Best Practice

Contracts should permit portfolio use after project completion unless confidentiality concerns exist.

## Risk Indicators

Medium Risk:

* Permanent prohibition on portfolio use.

---

# Confidentiality

Freelancers may receive confidential information.

Confidentiality clauses should define:

* Protected information.
* Exceptions.
* Duration.

## Best Practice

Reasonable confidentiality periods include:

* 2 years.
* 3 years.
* 5 years.

## Risk Indicators

High Risk:

* Broad perpetual confidentiality obligations.

---

# Independent Contractor Status

The agreement should clearly state:

* Freelancer is not an employee.
* Freelancer is responsible for taxes.
* No employment benefits are provided.

---

# Non-Compete Clauses

Freelancer contracts occasionally contain non-compete restrictions.

## Best Practice

Restrictions should be narrow and reasonable.

## Risk Indicators

Critical Risk:

* Prohibition on working with any competitor.
* Industry-wide restrictions.
* Long-term restrictions after contract completion.

---

# Non-Solicitation Clauses

These clauses restrict direct solicitation of employees or customers.

## Best Practice

Restrictions should be limited in duration and scope.

## Risk Indicators

High Risk:

* Unlimited duration.
* Excessively broad definitions.

---

# Indemnity Clauses

Indemnity provisions allocate legal risk.

## Balanced Standard

Each party indemnifies the other for losses caused by its own misconduct.

## Risk Indicators

Critical Risk:

* Unlimited one-sided indemnity.
* Indemnity for matters outside freelancer control.

---

# Limitation of Liability

Liability limitations are common commercial protections.

## Best Practice

Liability should be capped.

Example:

"Liability shall not exceed the total fees paid under this agreement."

## Risk Indicators

Critical Risk:

* Unlimited liability.

High Risk:

* Freelancer liability uncapped while client liability is capped.

---

# Termination Rights

Contracts should specify termination procedures.

## Common Standards

Termination:

* For convenience with notice.
* For breach.
* For non-payment.

## Best Practice

Notice periods of:

* 7 days.
* 14 days.
* 30 days.

are common.

## Risk Indicators

Critical Risk:

* Client may terminate immediately without compensation.

---

# Kill Fee

A kill fee compensates the freelancer when a project is cancelled after work has begun.

## Best Practice

Compensation should reflect work completed and reserved project time.

## Risk Indicators

High Risk:

* Client may cancel at any time without payment.

---

# Force Majeure

Force majeure clauses address extraordinary events beyond reasonable control.

Examples:

* Natural disasters.
* War.
* Government restrictions.
* Major infrastructure failures.

---

# Dispute Resolution

Contracts should specify:

* Governing law.
* Jurisdiction.
* Arbitration provisions.

## Best Practice

Disputes should be resolved in a practical and accessible forum.

## Risk Indicators

High Risk:

* Mandatory foreign litigation for low-value contracts.

---

# Contract Review Risk Scoring

Critical Risks:

* Unlimited liability.
* Unlimited indemnity.
* Broad IP assignment.
* Non-payment provisions.
* Unlimited revisions.
* Immediate termination without compensation.

High Risks:

* Missing payment schedule.
* Missing acceptance criteria.
* Broad non-compete clauses.
* Foreign jurisdiction requirements.

Medium Risks:

* Ambiguous deliverables.
* Vague timelines.
* Missing revision limits.

Low Risks:

* Minor drafting inconsistencies.
* Formatting issues.

---

# Freelancer-Friendly Contract Characteristics

A freelancer-friendly agreement typically includes:

* Clear scope of work.
* Defined deliverables.
* Milestone-based payments.
* Advance payment requirements.
* Limited revisions.
* Acceptance procedures.
* Liability caps.
* Balanced indemnity obligations.
* Portfolio rights.
* Reasonable termination provisions.
* Fair dispute resolution mechanisms.

---

# General Guidance

Freelancer agreements should balance commercial flexibility with legal certainty. Contracts that clearly define scope, payment obligations, ownership rights, liability allocation, and termination procedures generally present lower legal and commercial risk than contracts relying on broad discretionary powers or vague obligations.
